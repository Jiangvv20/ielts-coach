The Overlooked Pillar of Health: Sleep Science and Its Public Health Implications

For centuries, sleep was viewed as mere passivity — a biological necessity to be minimised wherever possible. The industrialisation of society only deepened this attitude; productivity culture glorified the ability to function on minimal rest, and the phrase "I'll sleep when I'm dead" became a cultural badge of honour. However, decades of research in chronobiology and sleep science have thoroughly dismantled these misconceptions, revealing sleep to be an active, dynamic process fundamental to virtually every system in the human body.

The circadian rhythm — the internal 24-hour biological clock — governs the timing of sleep and wakefulness, regulating hormonal secretion, body temperature, immune function, and cellular repair. Disruptions to this rhythm, whether caused by shift work, jet lag, or excessive screen time before bed, have been linked to a broad spectrum of health consequences. Research published in leading medical journals has shown that sleeping fewer than seven hours per night on a regular basis more than doubles the risk of developing hypertension and significantly impairs glucose regulation.

Sleep is also closely intertwined with immune competence. During deep sleep — specifically slow-wave sleep — the immune system releases cytokines, proteins that target infection and inflammation. Studies have demonstrated that sleep-deprived individuals exhibit markedly reduced natural killer cell activity, rendering them more susceptible to viral infection. One research team found that adults sleeping six hours or fewer per night were four times more likely to contract the common cold than those sleeping at least seven hours. The relationship extends to vaccine efficacy: subjects who slept fewer than six hours in the days following influenza vaccination produced antibody responses nearly 50% lower than well-rested counterparts.

Despite this evidence, sleep disorders remain dramatically underdiagnosed globally. Conditions such as obstructive sleep apnoea affect an estimated one billion adults worldwide, yet fewer than one in five sufferers receives a formal diagnosis or treatment. Untreated sleep apnoea is associated with a three-fold increase in the risk of stroke and contributes substantially to road traffic accidents and workplace injuries. Public health authorities in several high-income countries have begun incorporating sleep health into national wellness strategies; however, in lower-income settings, awareness remains limited.

Critics of expanded sleep health programmes argue that individual sleep habits are largely matters of personal responsibility and that public resources are better directed toward communicable diseases and acute care. Proponents counter that the economic burden attributable to sleep deficiency — including lost productivity, increased absenteeism, and higher healthcare utilisation — runs to hundreds of billions of dollars annually in major economies. They contend that targeted interventions in schools, workplaces, and primary care settings represent high-value investments in population health.

The science is unambiguous: sleep is not a luxury but a biological imperative. As awareness grows, public health infrastructure must evolve to reflect this understanding, integrating sleep medicine into routine preventive care and reducing the stigma that still surrounds conditions such as insomnia and hypersomnia. Only by treating sleep as the cornerstone of health that it truly is can societies hope to reduce the mounting burden of chronic disease.

Q01 · T/F/NG
Sleep was traditionally valued as an essential component of health in industrialised societies.
正确答案: B (False)
原文说工业化社会的"生产力文化"崇尚用最少睡眠运作，"I'll sleep when I'm dead"成为文化徽章，与"传统上重视睡眠"相矛盾。
Q02 · T/F/NG
The circadian rhythm regulates both hormonal secretion and immune function.
正确答案: A (True)
原文第二段直接说昼夜节律调节 "hormonal secretion, body temperature, immune function, and cellular repair"，与题目完全吻合。
Q03 · T/F/NG
Sleeping fewer than seven hours per night doubles the risk of developing type 2 diabetes.
正确答案: B (False)
原文说睡眠不足使高血压风险翻倍（hypertension），而非2型糖尿病（type 2 diabetes）。注意同义词陷阱，"impairs glucose regulation" 不等于"doubles diabetes risk"。
Q04 · T/F/NG
Adults sleeping six hours or fewer per night are four times more likely to contract the common cold.
正确答案: A (True)
原文第三段明确说 "adults sleeping six hours or fewer per night were four times more likely to contract the common cold"，与题目完全一致。
Q05 · T/F/NG
Sleep apnoea affects more women than men worldwide.
正确答案: C (Not Given)
原文只提到睡眠呼吸暂停影响约10亿成年人，未提及性别分布。不能用常识作答，原文无信息即 Not Given。
Q06 · T/F/NG
All countries have begun incorporating sleep health into their national wellness strategies.
正确答案: B (False)
原文说"several high-income countries"（几个高收入国家）已开始纳入睡眠健康，并指出低收入环境中意识仍然有限，与"所有国家"矛盾。注意绝对化词 all 是干扰信号。
Q07 · T/F/NG
Critics of sleep health programmes believe that individual sleep habits are influenced primarily by genetic factors.
正确答案: C (Not Given)
原文说批评者认为睡眠习惯是"personal responsibility"（个人责任），未提及遗传因素。遗传影响一词在文中完全未出现。
Q08 · 词汇填空
The body's internal 24-hour biological clock is known as the _____ rhythm. (中文: 昼夜节律的)
正确答案: circadian
circadian rhythm 是昼夜节律，源自拉丁语 circa dies（约一天）。IELTS 高频学术词汇，常与 disruption、clock 搭配。
Q09 · 词汇填空
During deep sleep, the immune system releases _____, proteins that target infection and inflammation. (中文: 细胞因子)
正确答案: cytokines
cytokines（细胞因子）是免疫系统分泌的信号蛋白，包括白细胞介素和肿瘤坏死因子。注意拼写：cyto-（细胞）+ kine（移动/活动）。也接受 cytokine（单数形式）。
Q10 · 词汇填空
A condition causing repeated interruptions in breathing during sleep, affecting approximately one billion adults, is obstructive sleep _____. (中文: 呼吸暂停)
正确答案: apnoea
apnoea（英式拼写）/ apnea（美式拼写）均可接受。源自希腊语 a-（无）+ pnoia（呼吸）。sleep apnoea 与 hypertension、stroke、daytime fatigue 强相关，是雅思常考健康话题词。
