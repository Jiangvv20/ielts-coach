Passage · The Rise of Slow Travel (~610 words)

The rise of slow travel as a philosophy and practice represents one of the most significant counter-trends to emerge from the age of budget airlines and frenetic package tourism. Rather than ticking destinations off a bucket list, slow travellers seek sustained immersion in a single place — staying for weeks or months rather than days, learning phrases of the local language, shopping in neighbourhood markets, and becoming, if only temporarily, a resident rather than a spectator.

The term “slow travel” draws from the broader Slow Movement, which originated in Italy in the late 1980s as a protest against the opening of a fast-food chain near the Spanish Steps in Rome. While philosopher and travel writer Alain de Botton has written extensively on the psychology of place and the quality of attention we bring to travel, the formal articulation of slow travel as a distinct category was largely the work of community-based tourism advocates in the early 2000s.

The environmental case for slow travel is compelling. A 2024 analysis published in the journal Nature Climate Change found that slow travellers — defined as those spending more than three weeks at a single destination — produced on average 40% fewer greenhouse gas emissions per day of travel than conventional tourists. The principal reason is the dramatic reduction in long-haul flights, which generate the greatest carbon footprint per kilometre of any mode of transport. A slow traveller who takes the train across Europe and stays for a month in one city can achieve a carbon footprint several orders of magnitude smaller than a fortnight of frequent long-haul flights between unconnected destinations.

Yet slow travel is not without its critics. The most persistent objection is one of privilege: the ability to spend weeks or months in a foreign country presupposes both financial security and the flexibility of remote work or extended leave. Scholars of tourism equity have argued that slow travel, despite its progressive credentials, is in practice accessible only to a narrow demographic — the educated, the globally mobile, and the financially comfortable.

The economic implications for host communities are, however, broadly positive. Slow travellers, by virtue of their extended stays, tend to spend more money in local establishments — family-run guesthouses, neighbourhood restaurants, and artisan workshops — rather than the large international chains that characterise resort tourism. A 2025 UNESCO report on regenerative tourism found that destinations in Portugal and Japan that had adopted slow tourism frameworks reported increased revenue for small local businesses and improved relationships between residents and visitors compared to pre-framework baselines.

There are psychological dividends too. Research by social psychologist Adam Galinsky has suggested that extended exposure to a foreign culture cultivates what he calls “integrative complexity” — the cognitive ability to hold multiple, sometimes contradictory perspectives simultaneously. Galinsky found that travellers who spent significant time living abroad, as opposed to simply visiting, scored higher on measures of creative thinking and cross-cultural empathy.

The movement faces structural obstacles. Modern working patterns, dominated by short annual leave entitlements and the digital always-on culture, make extended travel difficult for most workers. Even as remote working has freed some from geographic constraints, the cultural expectation of constant availability continues to erode the quality of attention that slow travel demands. Whether slow travel can transition from a niche practice of the privileged few to a mainstream alternative will depend as much on changes in workplace culture as on individual consumer choices.

Q01 · T/F/NG
Slow travellers typically aim to visit as many different destinations as possible within a short period of time.
✅ 正确答案: B (False)
原文第一段: “Rather than ticking destinations off a bucket list, slow travellers seek sustained immersion in a single place” — 慢旅行者追求的是在一处深度停留，与题目描述（尽量多访景点）完全相反。

According to a 2024 study, slow travellers produce fewer greenhouse gas emissions per day of travel compared with conventional tourists.
✅ 正确答案: A (True)
原文第三段: “slow travellers…produced on average 40% fewer greenhouse gas emissions per day of travel than conventional tourists.” 与题目完全吻合。

Q08 · 词汇填空
Slow travel emerged as a counter-trend to the ______ pace of modern package tourism, which prioritises the quantity of destinations visited over the depth of experience in any single place.
✅ 正确答案: frenetic
原文第一段: “the age of budget airlines and frenetic package tourism.” Frenetic = 狂乱的、疯狂紧张的，形容现代套票旅游的节奏。
