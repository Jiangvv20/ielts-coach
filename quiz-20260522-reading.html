Passage · AI Tutors and the Future of Personalised Education

The rise of artificial-intelligence tutoring systems has reignited debate over the role of human teachers. Proponents argue that AI tutors, capable of adapting in real time to a learner's pace and gaps, can deliver something traditional classrooms have always struggled to provide: genuine one-on-one instruction at scale. A landmark 2024 trial at a university in Ohio found that students using an AI tutor as a supplement scored, on average, 14% higher on standardised assessments than peers in conventional lectures. Crucially, the most pronounced gains were among historically underperforming groups, narrowing — though by no means eliminating — long-standing achievement gaps.

Critics, however, caution against over-enthusiasm. Education researcher Dr Lila Martinez notes that AI tutors excel at procedural knowledge but tend to flounder when students need help with abstract reasoning, ethical judgement, or creative synthesis. Moreover, the data appetite of these systems raises privacy concerns: every keystroke, hesitation, and misclick is logged. Several jurisdictions have already moved to regulate how such data may be stored and shared. Despite the technology's promise, most experts agree that AI should be positioned as a complement to skilled teachers, not a replacement.
