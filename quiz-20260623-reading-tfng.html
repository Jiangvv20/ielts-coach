Passage · Platform Regulation and the Future of Online Speech

The governance of online platforms has emerged as one of the defining policy battles of the 2020s. At the centre of this debate is a fundamental tension: how to curb the harms that large social-media companies can amplify — misinformation, hate speech, radicalisation — without simultaneously chilling legitimate expression. Two jurisdictions have adopted markedly different regulatory philosophies, and their divergence is reshaping global norms.

In the United States, Section 230 of the Communications Decency Act has for decades granted platforms broad immunity from liability for third-party content. Critics argue this blanket protection incentivises inaction on harmful content. Reform efforts have repeatedly stalled in Congress, however, with legislators unable to agree on whether the priority should be to hold platforms more accountable for extremist material or to prevent what some conservatives call anti-conservative censorship. The result is an effective regulatory standstill.

The European Union has taken a more interventionist path. The Digital Services Act (DSA), which entered into force in 2023, creates a tiered system of obligations: all platforms must remove illegal content expeditiously, but additional "systemic risk" duties — including mandatory algorithmic audits and independent researcher data access — apply only to the largest "very large online platforms" (VLOPs) such as Meta, TikTok, and X. This size-tiered approach distinguishes the DSA sharply from the American model. Crucially, the DSA replaces the old blanket immunity inherited from the EU's 2000 E-Commerce Directive with a conditional safe harbour: platforms retain protection only if they act with due diligence upon receiving notice of illegal content. Failure to do so exposes them to liability.

Critics of the DSA, however, caution that the law's broad content-moderation obligations risk creating a chilling effect on legitimate speech. If platforms face heavy fines for hosting borderline content, they may err on the side of over-removal, silencing minority viewpoints and political dissent. Civil-liberties groups have pointed to early evidence of over-blocking in the months following the DSA's rollout, though regulators contest this characterisation.

Underlying both regimes is a deeper structural critique articulated by scholars such as Shoshana Zuboff: that the business model of major platforms is built on what she terms surveillance capitalism — the systematic extraction and monetisation of users' behavioural data to predict and influence behaviour. This model, Zuboff argues, is incompatible with genuine democratic deliberation, because it structurally incentivises engagement over accuracy, outrage over nuance. Regulatory frameworks that focus only on content moderation, without addressing the underlying business model, may therefore treat symptoms rather than causes.

Whether the DSA model will become a global template — the so-called "Brussels Effect," whereby EU regulations de facto set international standards — or whether a genuinely international framework for platform governance will emerge, remains to be seen. Experts are divided, and the outcome will likely depend on how vigorously the European Commission enforces the DSA in its early years.

Q01 · T/F/NG
The DSA applies identical regulatory obligations to all online platforms regardless of their size.
B · False
原文: "creates a tiered system of obligations … additional 'systemic risk' duties … apply only to the largest 'very large online platforms'." DSA 按规模分层施责, 并非一视同仁。
Q02 · T/F/NG
Under the DSA, platforms lose immunity from liability if they fail to act diligently on notices of illegal content.
A · True
原文: "platforms retain protection only if they act with due diligence upon receiving notice … Failure to do so exposes them to liability." 条件性安全港, 不履行义务则失去豁免。
Q03 · T/F/NG
Civil-liberties groups have raised concerns that the DSA could lead to excessive removal of legitimate content.
A · True
原文: "Civil-liberties groups have pointed to early evidence of over-blocking in the months following the DSA's rollout." 对过度删除合法内容的担忧有明确表述。
Q04 · T/F/NG
The DSA directly regulates the architecture of platform recommendation algorithms.
B · False
原文要求的是"mandatory algorithmic audits"(算法审计), 而非直接规定算法架构本身。审计 ≠ 规定架构设计, 与题目"directly regulates the architecture"矛盾。
Q05 · T/F/NG
Attempts to reform Section 230 in the United States have so far been unsuccessful.
A · True
原文: "Reform efforts have repeatedly stalled in Congress … The result is an effective regulatory standstill." 改革屡次受阻, 即"unsuccessful"。
