Day 55 · 11 题 · ~12 分钟

Passage · Urban Exploration and Cross-Cultural Exchange in the Modern City (~620 words)

The contemporary city has become the world’s most complex laboratory for cross-cultural exchange. Unlike previous generations, when international encounters were largely confined to formal diplomatic settings or colonial contact zones, today’s urban traveller moves fluidly between neighbourhoods, cuisines, languages, and social norms, often within the span of a single afternoon. A 2025 survey by the UNWTO found that cosmopolitan city destinations now account for 62% of all international overnight stays, with travellers citing cultural diversity as the primary draw in 43% of cases.

Much of this exchange is concentrated in what urban geographers call “creative quarters” — districts that were once industrial or working-class, subsequently colonised by artists and young professionals, and eventually transformed by rising property values and boutique commerce. Sociologists describe this process as gentrification: the displacement of lower-income residents and businesses by wealthier incomers, typically following a pattern of aesthetic renovation that increases the cultural cachet and economic value of an area. While gentrification attracts tourism by producing the distinctive streetscapes, independent cafés, and art galleries that travellers find appealing, it simultaneously erases the lived cultural diversity it is marketed on.

Navigating urban cultural exchange also demands attention to etiquette: the unspoken rules that govern appropriate behaviour in public and semi-public spaces. The same act — photographing a stranger, bargaining in a shop, or sitting uninvited at a table already occupied — may be considered entirely normal in one culture and deeply offensive in another. Travel writers and sociologists have long emphasised that respectful cross-cultural interaction begins not with language acquisition but with observation: watching how local people greet each other, how they queue, and how they treat service workers provides more reliable social intelligence than any guidebook.

Cities that successfully host cultural exchange tend to foster a sense of camaraderie among visitors and residents alike — a spirit of shared urban experience that transcends national boundaries. Studies of long-term expatriate communities in cities such as Singapore, Dubai, and Amsterdam suggest that this sense of belonging develops most readily in spaces designed for informal encounter: public markets, community gardens, libraries, and pedestrian-friendly streets. Conversely, urban environments characterised by gated communities, car dependency, and privatised public space tend to produce social atomisation rather than cultural integration.

The arts play an increasingly prominent role in facilitating cross-cultural urban experiences. Municipal investment in street art, public sculpture, and cultural festivals has been shown in multiple studies to increase footfall in previously neglected districts, generate economic multipliers across hospitality and retail, and measurably improve residents’ sense of civic identity. The Tate Modern in London, the Guggenheim Bilbao in Spain, and the MASP in São Paulo are frequently cited as landmark examples of cultural institutions that have catalysed broader urban regeneration — though critics note that such “flagship” cultural projects often primarily benefit higher-income visitors rather than the local communities they nominally serve.

The challenge for 21st-century cities is therefore to design urban environments that enable genuine cultural encounter rather than merely curating cultural difference for tourist consumption. This requires policies that protect socioeconomic diversity — through affordable housing, small-business grants, and inclusive public space — while simultaneously celebrating the architectural layering, multilingual street life, and artistic vitality that make cities magnets for international visitors. A city that prices out its artists and working-class communities in the pursuit of a sanitised “global” appeal may find that it has destroyed the authentic cultural texture that attracted visitors in the first place.

Q01 · T/F/NG
According to the 2025 UNWTO survey, cosmopolitan city destinations account for more than half of all international overnight stays.
✅ 正确答案: A (True)
原文第一段: “cosmopolitan city destinations now account for 62% of all international overnight stays.” 62% 超过一半，与题目 “more than half” 完全吻合。
Q02 · T/F/NG
The gentrification of urban districts typically begins when wealthy investors deliberately purchase properties to displace existing low-income residents.
✅ 正确答案: B (False)
原文第二段描述的顺序是: 先由艺术家和年轻专业人士入驻，然后才因房价上涨而排挤低收入居民。题目称 “begins when wealthy investors deliberately purchase properties”，与原文的 “colonised by artists and young professionals” 直接矛盾。
Q03 · T/F/NG

Q08 · 词汇填空
A 2025 UNWTO survey found that ______ city destinations — celebrated for their cultural diversity and multilingual street life — now account for 62% of all international overnight stays.
✅ 正确答案: cosmopolitan
原文第一段: “cosmopolitan city destinations now account for 62% of all international overnight stays.” Cosmopolitan = 国际化的、兼容并蓄的，形容城市具有来自世界各地的多元文化特质。
Q09 · 词汇填空
The process by which rising property values and wealthier incomers gradually displace lower-income residents from formerly working-class urban districts is known as ______.
✅ 正确答案: gentrification
原文第二段: “Sociologists describe this process as gentrification: the displacement of lower-income residents and businesses by wealthier incomers.” Gentrification = 中产阶级化/绅士化，指高收入群体涌入导致原有低收入社区被取代的现象。
Q10 · 词汇填空
The author argues that understanding local ______ — the unspoken rules governing appropriate behaviour in public spaces — is as important as language skills for respectful cross-cultural interaction.
✅ 正确答案: etiquette
原文第三段: “Navigating urban cultural exchange also demands attention to etiquette: the unspoken rules that govern appropriate behaviour in public and semi-public spaces.” Etiquette = 礼仪、行为规范，尤指特定社会或场合中约定俗成的行为准则。
Q11 · 词汇填空
Successful multicultural cities tend to foster a sense of ______ among both residents and visitors, a spirit of shared urban experience that transcends national or cultural boundaries.
✅ 正确答案: camaraderie
原文第四段: “Cities that successfully host cultural exchange tend to foster a sense of camaraderie among visitors and residents alike — a spirit of shared urban experience that transcends national boundaries.” Camaraderie = 友情、同伴情谊，指因共同经历而产生的亲密感与归属感。
