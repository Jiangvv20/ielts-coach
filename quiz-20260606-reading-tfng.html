Surveillance Technology and the Modern City: Balancing Safety and Privacy

In cities around the world, a quiet transformation is under way. Advanced cameras, facial recognition software and networked sensors are being deployed on an unprecedented scale, promising safer streets and more efficient public services. Proponents argue that these technologies represent a natural evolution of urban management — a logical extension of the CCTV systems that have existed for decades. Critics, however, warn that we are crossing a threshold into a world of pervasive surveillance, in which the collection of personal data by both governments and corporations fundamentally undermines civil liberties.

The concept of the "smart city" is central to this debate. Cities such as Singapore, Dubai and several municipalities in China have embraced sensor-laden infrastructure that monitors traffic patterns, air quality and crowd density in real time. In some cases, facial recognition systems scan pedestrians at transport hubs, automatically flagging individuals on watchlists. Advocates point to measurable results: London's implementation of congestion-charging cameras reduced traffic by an estimated 30 per cent, while predictive policing tools in Los Angeles reportedly cut certain categories of crime in specific neighbourhoods.

Yet the benefits come with undeniable trade-offs. A landmark study published in 2024 found that facial recognition systems from leading technology vendors had error rates of up to 35 per cent for darker-skinned individuals, compared with just one per cent for lighter-skinned men. Civil rights organisations argue that these disparities effectively encode racial bias into public safety infrastructure, disproportionately exposing minority communities to wrongful suspicion. Several cities in the United States — including San Francisco and Boston — have responded by banning municipal use of facial recognition technology altogether.

The European Union has sought a middle path. Its Artificial Intelligence Act, which entered into force in 2024, prohibits real-time remote biometric identification in public spaces in all but a narrow set of circumstances, such as the search for missing children or the prevention of terrorist attacks. Data protection advocates welcomed the legislation but noted significant loopholes, arguing that private companies operating in retail environments are subject to far looser restrictions than their public-sector counterparts.

Central to the regulatory debate is the concept of informed consent. Many people remain largely unaware of the extent to which their movements, transactions and social interactions generate digital traces that are collected, stored and monetised. The "right to be forgotten," enshrined in EU law since 2016, grants citizens the legal right to request the deletion of personal data under specific conditions, yet enforcement has proved patchy. Research suggests that fewer than 40 per cent of Europeans are aware that the right exists, let alone how to exercise it.

Looking ahead, the pace of technological change threatens to outstrip the capacity of regulatory frameworks to keep up. Emerging tools such as gait analysis software — which identifies individuals from the way they walk — do not require subjects to face a camera at all, rendering many existing privacy safeguards effectively obsolete. Meanwhile, the integration of artificial intelligence with urban sensor networks is enabling a form of predictive governance that some scholars describe as moving from policing crime to policing intent.

Whether smart city technology ultimately serves its citizens or subjects them to a new form of digital authoritarianism may depend less on the technology itself than on the strength of democratic institutions and the willingness of governments to subject powerful tools to genuine public audit. For now, the debate continues — and the cameras keep watching.

Part A · True / False / Not Given   (8 题)
根据文章内容判断：T = 文中明确支持 · F = 文中明确反驳 · NG = 文中未提及
Q1 · T / F / NG
According to the passage, smart city surveillance technology is an entirely new phenomenon with no connection to earlier urban management practices.
FALSE 原文依据: "a logical extension of the CCTV systems that have existed for decades" — 文章明确表明该技术是现有闭路电视系统的延伸，并非全新事物。
IELTS tip: 注意题目中的极端词 "entirely new" — 原文说的是"logical extension"，直接矛盾。
Q2 · T / F / NG
The 2024 landmark study found that facial recognition error rates were significantly higher for darker-skinned individuals than for lighter-skinned men.
TRUE 原文依据: "error rates of up to 35 per cent for darker-skinned individuals, compared with just one per cent for lighter-skinned men" — 题目表述与原文完全吻合。
IELTS tip: 数字类细节是 TRUE/FALSE 的常见考点，原文比率与题目一致即为 TRUE。
Q3 · T / F / NG
London introduced facial recognition systems that successfully reduced urban traffic by approximately 30 per cent.
FALSE 原文依据: "London's implementation of congestion-charging cameras reduced traffic by an estimated 30 per cent" — 是收费摄像头而非人脸识别系统。题目张冠李戴，故为 FALSE。
IELTS tip: FALSE 题常将原文中的两个概念混淆。要仔细区分 "congestion-charging cameras" 与 "facial recognition systems"。
Q4 · T / F / NG
The EU's Artificial Intelligence Act completely prohibits all forms of biometric identification in public spaces without any exceptions.
FALSE 原文依据: "prohibits real-time remote biometric identification in public spaces in all but a narrow set of circumstances" — 存在例外情况（如寻找失踪儿童、预防恐怖袭击），并非完全禁止。
IELTS tip: "completely... without any exceptions" vs 原文 "in all but a narrow set of circumstances" — 绝对化表达是 FALSE 的强烈信号。
Q5 · T / F / NG
Fewer than half of European citizens are aware of their right to request deletion of their personal data.
TRUE 原文依据: "fewer than 40 per cent of Europeans are aware that the right exists" — 40% 少于 50%（即 "fewer than half"），题目表述与原文逻辑一致。
IELTS tip: 注意数量换算：文中 "fewer than 40 per cent" → 题目 "fewer than half (50%)"，需作简单数学判断。
Q6 · T / F / NG
Gait analysis technology is ineffective unless the subject looks directly at a camera during identification.
FALSE 原文依据: "gait analysis software — which identifies individuals from the way they walk — do not require subjects to face a camera at all" — 正好与题目相反。
IELTS tip: 文中否定词 "do not require" 与题目肯定表述直接矛盾 → FALSE。
Q7 · T / F / NG
The passage concludes that smart city technology will inevitably result in some form of digital authoritarianism.
NOT GIVEN 原文依据: "Whether smart city technology ultimately serves its citizens or subjects them to... authoritarianism may depend on..." — 原文用的是条件句和不确定表达，并未作出 "inevitable（必然）" 的结论性判断。
IELTS tip: NG 常见于原文使用了 "may / might / could / depending on" 等条件表达，而题目却将其表述为确定结果。
Q8 · T / F / NG
Private companies operating in retail environments in the EU face the same level of facial recognition restrictions as public-sector organisations.
FALSE 原文依据: "private companies operating in retail environments are subject to far looser restrictions than their public-sector counterparts" — 明确说明私营企业受到的限制宽松得多。
IELTS tip: 比较级是 FALSE 题的重要考点。"far looser restrictions" 与题目 "same level" 直接矛盾。
Part B · 词汇填空   (4 题)
根据上下文和文章词汇填入最合适的词 (今日词卡 150 词范围内)
Q9 · 词汇填空
Ensuring the ___________ of sensitive health data — knowing exactly where it originated and who has accessed it — is a cornerstone of responsible data governance.
✓ ANSWER 答案: provenance
释义: 数据来源/溯源 — 知道数据从何而来、谁曾访问过。
搭配: data provenance · verify provenance · chain of provenance
例句: Blockchain technology can verify data provenance, making digital records tamper-resistant.
Q10 · 词汇填空
A growing number of local authorities are conducting a security ___________ of their surveillance systems to ensure they comply with the new data protection regulations.
✓ ANSWER 答案: audit
释义: 审计 — 对系统或实践进行系统性检查。
搭配: security audit · conduct an audit · independent audit
例句: An independent audit uncovered several undisclosed data-sharing practices hidden in the platform's code.
Q11 · 词汇填空
As gait analysis and other new technologies emerge, many existing privacy safeguards risk becoming effectively ___________, failing to protect citizens from new forms of identification.
✓ ANSWER 答案: obsolete
释义: 过时的 — 不再有效或适用的。
搭配: render obsolete · become obsolete · effectively obsolete
例句: Rapid innovation risks rendering many current professional skills completely obsolete within a decade.
Q12 · 词汇填空
To prevent regulatory failures, governments must ___________ the risks of deploying AI-powered surveillance by requiring mandatory impact assessments and public consultations before any new system goes live.
✓ ANSWER 答案: mitigate
释义: 减轻/缓解 — 采取措施降低某种风险或危害的程度。
搭配: mitigate risks · mitigate the impact · mitigate harm
例句: Regular automated backups help organisations mitigate the risks associated with ransomware attacks.
