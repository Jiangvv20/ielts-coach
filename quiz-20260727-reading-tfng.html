进划 · Passage (600+ words)

The Global Freshwater Crisis: Scarcity, Overuse, and the Path Forward

Of all the water on Earth, approximately 97.5 per cent is saltwater found in the oceans. The remaining 2.5 per cent is freshwater, but roughly 70 per cent of that is locked in glaciers and ice caps, rendering it practically inaccessible. Only around 0.3 per cent of all freshwater on Earth is found in surface sources — rivers, lakes, and reservoirs — that are directly accessible for human use. Groundwater stored in underground aquifers accounts for most of the rest, yet extraction rates in many regions far exceed the natural recharge rate, meaning that once depleted, these reserves may take thousands of years to recover.

The Ogallala Aquifer beneath the Great Plains of the United States exemplifies this challenge. Since large-scale irrigation began in the mid-twentieth century, water levels in parts of the aquifer have dropped by more than 60 metres. Scientists project that, at current extraction rates, economically viable portions of the aquifer could be exhausted within 50 years, threatening the agricultural output of a region that produces a significant share of the world's wheat and beef. Similar stories of unsustainable groundwater use are emerging in northern India, the Middle East, and parts of northern China.

Climate change is compounding the crisis. Rising global temperatures are altering precipitation patterns, causing some regions to experience intensified droughts while others face more severe flooding. Glacial retreat is particularly alarming: billions of people in South and Central Asia depend on glacial meltwater for seasonal freshwater supplies, and as glaciers shrink, this reliable source is diminishing. Sea-level rise is simultaneously threatening coastal freshwater reserves through salinisation — the intrusion of saltwater into groundwater and agricultural land.

Demand-side pressures are equally daunting. Agriculture accounts for roughly 70 per cent of global freshwater withdrawals, with much of that water applied inefficiently through flood irrigation. Industrial processes and cooling systems for power plants consume a further 20 per cent. Municipal water systems, which serve urban households, account for only around 10 per cent — yet urban populations are the most vocal in demanding solutions, creating a political dynamic that often diverts attention from agricultural overuse.

A range of technological and policy solutions exists. Drip irrigation, which delivers water directly to plant roots, can reduce agricultural water consumption by 30 to 60 per cent compared with traditional flood irrigation, and its adoption has expanded considerably in water-scarce regions such as Israel and Spain. Desalination — the removal of salt from seawater to produce potable water — has become economically viable in energy-rich coastal nations, though it remains expensive and energy-intensive compared with conventional water sources. Water pricing mechanisms, which charge consumers based on actual use, have consistently proved effective at reducing waste; cities that have introduced tiered pricing structures have seen per-capita consumption fall by 15 to 25 per cent.

On the international front, shared river systems frequently generate geopolitical tension. The Nile, the Mekong, and the Jordan each flow through multiple nations with competing claims on their waters, and as climate-induced scarcity intensifies, disputes over allocation are likely to escalate. Legal frameworks for managing transboundary rivers remain incomplete, and some analysts warn that water scarcity could become a significant driver of conflict in the coming decades. Others, however, point to successful cooperation agreements — such as the Indus Waters Treaty between India and Pakistan, which has held for over 60 years despite repeated political crises — as evidence that states can and do find pragmatic solutions to shared resource challenges.

Ultimately, solving the freshwater crisis requires a paradigm shift: treating water as the finite, irreplaceable resource it is rather than a commodity to be exploited without regard for future generations. This shift demands investment in technology, reform of agricultural incentives, stronger international governance, and a change in public attitudes that places water conservation at the heart of sustainable development.

Q01 · T/F/NG
Approximately 2.5 per cent of all water on Earth is freshwater, and most of it is directly accessible in rivers and lakes.
正确答案: B (False)
原文说 2.5% 是液水（正确），但直接可用的仅就是 0.3% — 目称 "most of it" 可直接获取与原文相矩。
Q02 · T/F/NG
The Ogallala Aquifer has experienced a drop of more than 60 metres in some areas since intensive irrigation began.
正确答案: A (True)
原文直接诵: "water levels in parts of the aquifer have dropped by more than 60 metres." 完全支持。
Q03 · T/F/NG
The Ogallala Aquifer will be completely exhausted within the next 20 years if current extraction rates continue.
正确答案: C (Not Given)
原文说 "within 50 years", 而且是 "economically viable portions" 结束，不是 "completely exhausted within 20 years"。两点都与题目不符。
Q04 · T/F/NG
Agriculture is responsible for the largest share of global freshwater withdrawals.
正确答案: A (True)
原文诵: "Agriculture accounts for roughly 70 per cent of global freshwater withdrawals." 最大份额，完全支持。
Q05 · T/F/NG
Desalination is cheaper and less energy-intensive than conventional water supply methods.
正确答案: B (False)
原文诵 desalination "remains expensive and energy-intensive compared with conventional water sources" — 与题目集直接相矩。
Q06 · T/F/NG
Cities that have implemented tiered water pricing have seen per-capita consumption decrease.
正确答案: A (True)
原文诵: "cities that have introduced tiered pricing structures have seen per-capita consumption fall by 15 to 25 per cent." 桀收支持。
Q07 · T/F/NG
The Indus Waters Treaty has been widely praised by international legal scholars as the most successful water-sharing agreement in history.
正确答案: C (Not Given)
原文提到 Indus Waters Treaty 以说明合作是可能的，但从未请进国际法学学者的评价或"最成功"的见解。
Q08 · 词汇填空
An underground layer of water-bearing rock that holds and transmits groundwater is called an ____. (含水层)
正确答案: aquifer
aquifer = 含水层。据 Band 7+ 高频词汇，敏无关的持综業纳的划公姐协策中閯的关键词。
Q09 · 词汇填空
The process by which soil or groundwater becomes increasingly salty, often due to seawater intrusion, is called ____. (盒確化)
正确答案: salinisation (也接受 salinization)
salinisation = 土地/地下水盒確化。原文中加粗: "salinisation — the intrusion of saltwater into groundwater and agricultural land."
Q10 · 找拷填空
The technology used to remove salt from seawater to make it drinkable is known as ____. (海水涱化)
正确答案: desalination
desalination = 海水涱化。原文诵: "Desalination — the removal of salt from seawater to produce potable water."
